Project 03

RelayPlay

A personalized weekend-recommendation service, designed and built end to end.

Role Founder, CEO, Designer Company San Diego, 2020–2022 Outcome ~300 subscribers, ~15K followers

Local expert profiles and personalized recommendations. UX/UI designed in Figma; React platform built by an engineering partner from those specs.

Problem

Weekend planning meant thirty browser tabs across Eventbrite, Meetup, and Airbnb Experiences — and by the time a decision got made, tickets were sold out. Algorithmic recommendation apps couldn't replicate the warmth of a friend's suggestion.

HCI Approach

Built a human-in-the-loop model: local experts curate, software delivers at scale. Designed every screen in Figma so the delivery moment — five picks, every Friday — felt like a personal note from a friend, not a marketplace browse.

User Flow
Subscribe → Friday curation arrives → Follow an expert or request one → Book the experience
Outcome

Grew from 85 to ~300 subscribers and ~15,000 Instagram followers. Validated that people pay for curation that feels personal — but COVID collapsed the event economy before the business model was solved.

Reflection

Should have proven the unit economics — price, curation cost, margin — before building a single screen. People loved the product. The money never followed, and I never answered that question early enough for it to matter.

Problem framing

I'd get to Friday evening with no idea what to do with my weekend, open a browser, and have thirty tabs open within ten minutes across SD Reader, Facebook Events, Eventbrite, Meetup, and Airbnb Experiences. By the time I narrowed it down, tickets were sold out. I knew people who weren't like this — friends who seemed to know about every cool thing happening in the city. That asymmetry was the market gap.

Research backed the instinct: 72% of millennials preferred spending on experiences over things, 69% reported FOMO when they couldn't attend events. The demand was real. Discovery infrastructure was the bottleneck.

The product isn't an event aggregator. The product is a personal relationship with a knowledgeable curator, delivered through software.

Human-in-the-loop, by design

Most weekend-discovery products in 2020 bet on pure algorithmic recommendation. That bet underestimated the cold-start problem — a new user has no signal — and underestimated the value of taste itself. A great recommendation isn't a statistical match; it's an act of interpretation. We took on the cost of human curation in exchange for warmth the competition couldn't match.

From Friday delivery to a curator platform

The product started as simply as possible: a landing page, a Thursday email with a PowerPoint of five curated experiences, no app, no algorithm. The point was validating demand before building anything — and the delivery moment itself, five picks with expert notes, became the design anchor everything else hung from.

Centralized curation became a bottleneck to growth, so the product evolved into a web app where experts built their own collections by category and users could follow a specific curator or request one directly.

Build and execution

I designed the full UX/UI in Figma — every screen, state, and interaction — before AI-assisted design tooling existed, and partnered with a development team who built the React platform from those specs. I also led brand, the Instagram presence, acquisition, and the operational rhythm behind the weekly curation.

The business model we never solved

We assumed a $10/month subscription — clean and low-friction. It never covered the real cost of human curation. We tested ticketed events, brand partnerships, and premium tiers; none closed cleanly. Then COVID collapsed the event economy the product depended on entirely.

What I'd do differently

I treated the business model as something we'd figure out as we grew — the standard founder assumption that if people love it, the money follows. People loved RelayPlay. The money didn't follow. I should have answered "at what price does this cover curation and produce a margin" before building a single screen.
