Project 04

Intuit Business Formation

A service that makes complex entity-formation decisions feel approachable.

Role Staff Product Manager Company Intuit QuickBooks Live Launched Advisory Alpha, April 2026 Outcome $15–20M FY27 opportunity

Intuit QuickBooks Live — where the Business Formation service lives.

Problem

Forming a business (LLC, S-Corp, C-Corp) means dozens of consequential, one-time decisions most owners are making for the first time. Competitors like LegalZoom solve the paperwork. None of them solve the judgment call.

HCI Approach

Split the service into three layers — Advisory (human expert, differentiated), Filing (commoditized execution), Compliance (recurring relationship) — so customers pay a premium only for the judgment they actually need. Kept accounting cross-sell out of the flow until formation was decided, protecting attention for the decision that mattered.

User Flow
Segment-specific intake → 1:1 session with a CPA/EA → Personalized roadmap → Optional filing & compliance
Outcome

Advisory Alpha launched April 2026 across CA, NY, FL, and TX. Research validated 60% Top-2-Box interest among prospects, rising to 81% among those who'd used a similar service before. Sized at $15–20M of incremental FY27 revenue.

Reflection

The advisory-first sequencing is unproven at full scale. Beta (scheduled July 2026) will tell us whether the trust built in Advisory actually converts into Filing and Compliance attach — that's the open question I'm watching closest.

Problem framing

Starting a business in the U.S. is hard in a peculiar way: the information is public, the forms are free, but the path from "I'm starting a business" to "correctly formed, tax-optimized, and compliant" runs through dozens of decisions with multi-year financial consequences — usually made by someone doing it for the first time.

Research surfaced two friction layers: decision friction (which entity, what tax implications, what compliance) and filing-and-compliance friction (confusing government sites, error-prone forms, late fees). The quantitative signal validated the opportunity — 60% Top-2-Box interest among prospects, 81% among those who'd felt the pain before.

How do you design a service that makes complex legal and financial decisions feel approachable, without oversimplifying to the point of malpractice?

A three-layer service architecture

The industry default bundles everything into one price, obscuring where the value lives. We separated Advisory (a 1:1 session with a credentialed tax expert, producing a roadmap the customer keeps regardless of whether they file with us — where we built trust competitors couldn't match), Filing (necessary but not differentiating, delivered via partner integration), and Compliance (where trust becomes ongoing recurring value). Premium pricing for the judgment customers want; commoditized pricing for the mechanics they find tedious — the inverse of how legal services traditionally price.

Don't drown the new user

Most target customers didn't yet have a full QuickBooks Online subscription, and the instinct was to use Business Formation as a cross-sell moment. But customers mid-formation were cognitively maxed out on a multi-year legal decision — pushing accounting features into that moment would have signaled we cared more about cross-sell than their actual problem. We kept QBO features ungated but unsurfaced until advisory was complete.

Three audiences, three mental models

New prospects (1.5M eligible), sole-proprietors formalizing (238K), and existing LLC/Corp owners re-evaluating structure (57K) each got a distinct intake flow and expert prep brief, sharing the same underlying service infrastructure — each anxious about a different question, from "am I doing this right" to "am I leaving money on the table."

Phased validation with explicit leaps of faith

The launch is structured around gated stages (Dry Tests → Alpha → Beta → Launch), each testing a specific Leap-of-Faith Assumption with pre-committed Go/No-Go criteria — the same phased-validation discipline I used to launch Instant Ink Paper.
